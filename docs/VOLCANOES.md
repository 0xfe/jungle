# Rare active volcanoes

Press **V** to visit the nearest volcano. Ordinary exploration can find the same seeded sites. Four retained ImageGen structural forms share one atlas: a steep cone, broad open lava lake, twin mouths with only one active, and a broken caldera. Source art is `assets/source/volcano-forms.png`; the exact prompt, tool and SHA-256 are in `assets/volcano-prompts.json`. Builds remain offline. Nearest-neighbor baking keeps one registered pose per form; shared procedural smoke, fire, ash and surface materials animate the feature without extra textures per site.

## Placement and ground

`src/jungle/volcanoes.ts` chooses one candidate in each 160-tile cell, accepting 22% of cells. Bounded jitter guarantees at least **112 tiles** between sites; expected density is roughly one volcano per 116,000 tiles. The initial 40-tile area around world coordinate zero is excluded. Each cone is about five tiles across, with a short lava river at its foot and sparse outer vegetation reaching roughly eight tiles. Site selection is stateless, reproducible and independent of camera, visit order or chunk ownership. Signed coordinates use floor division.

A shared radial scar raises the lake field while preserving connected river channels, biases the ground toward dry land and removes canopy near the cone. Sparse grass and bushes bridge its outer edge into jungle. Ordinary terrain sliders do not remove volcanic scars. Dust, ash and porous rubble overlays follow the same triangulated terrain height as actors. Cone clearance prevents ground animals walking through the mountain; low vegetation remains traversable. Sites overlapping the active region are reconstructed from seed, with no visited-site history.

## Lava and eruptions

Each form has one short, already-filled lava river, about 1.9–2.25 tiles from its painted outlet to its rounded terminal pool. `VOLCANO_OUTLETS` registers the outlet position, width and direction against the 104×88 registration coordinates (now baked at 312×264 texels) and shared logical scale. The inlet overlaps the final painted slope pixels and blends its height onto local ground, avoiding detached streams. Curved banks vary in width and widen into a bulbous end. A continuous orange/yellow molten material, irregular dark rock islands and a crust bank share the mountain's palette. Sixty-four red/orange/yellow-white pixel trails travel in varied lanes and speeds across this established surface, with small rising smoke wisps. There are no advancing or resetting lava fronts.

The retained source is now sampled at **288×240 inside a 312×264 canvas**, three times the previous linear resolution (nine times the texels), with the same logical size and anchors. The offline baker traces up to 72 short descending routes through incandescent source pixels and retains 160 glow samples per form. Moving three-pixel tails follow those routes; a travelling heat wave brightens the painted channels and active crater. The inactive twin mouth has no hot source pixels and receives no flow effects. Source registration is shared immutable manifest data; runtime never reads back the atlas or allocates per-volcano textures.

`CONFIG.world.volcanoes.lavaFlowSpeed` sets river travel cycles per second (default 0.1); short slope paths use faster cycles. `lavaParticles` controls ground trail count (64); `lavaSmoke` controls small wisps on each surface (8). All effects derive from interpolated world time and seeded per-particle offsets, freeze on pause, and leave simulation RNG/state untouched. The underlying river remains filled.

Rendering and contact share **48 static segments** per volcano. Geometry memoization is capped at eight entries and is independent of simulation/presentation time. The crust renders first, then the molten surface, with explicit painter depths at the foot. All effects use the existing transparent atlas and painter stream. Registered mouth smoke rises continuously; seventeen-second spurt windows occasionally throw larger, faster explosive clasts. Scene time interpolates fixed 60 Hz samples and freezes on pause. The twin volcano's inactive mouth stays dark.

These are artistic channels, not a fluid, erosion or geological simulation. The dark margins represent cooling ash/obsidian without accumulating permanent terrain edits. Clasts are visual effects; contact uses the ground river. Water outside the scar retains the normal physical terrain field.

## Wildlife and replacements

The outer volcanic belt multiplies whole-group candidate probability by 1.8; species-local RNG, solitary predators and family sizes remain intact. `VolcanicWildlifeAgent` (type **63**) owns a ground/canopy animal's lifecycle near a site, its replacement template and one recent ash pile. Water and airborne ecological species keep their ordinary behavior. Animals above eight logical pixels are safe from ground contact; grounded monkeys/apes and climbing species can react when low enough.

Ordinary animal target selection and motor steps reject the lava bank and inner ash bed, using species terrain/habitat rules. Initial ground spawning uses the same exclusion. The sparse outer belt stays traversable, so wildlife remains nearby. Animals already inside may move outward to escape, but not deeper into the exclusion. Their existing motor still turns, accelerates and advances gait by actual distance. A site offers one adventurous walking excursion roughly every 10–20 active seconds. Before updates, a deterministic planner nominates a nearby deer, zebra, wolf, boar, black bear, giraffe or jaguar with a clear, habitat-valid route to the visible terminal pool. Other animals retain avoidance. The nominee owns its target and timeout, uses its ordinary turning/walking motor at a brisk pace, and may cross its owner chunk edge within a bounded expanded territory. No actor is teleported onto lava. The terminal pool is excluded from the solid mountain collision disk.

A nominated walker bursts into flames after 0.25 seconds of actual hot contact. The cadence is a target for populated, reachable sites: travel time varies, blocked routes defer nominations, and depleted populations wait for off-screen replacements. Slot/target/remaining-trip state is checkpointed; planning runs at most once per simulation second and is independent of agent update order. Sleeping trips resume with their remaining active time. Blocked escape can still cause an incidental loss. Failed escape searches retry after 0.6–1 seconds, not every tick. For ordinary animals, 2.25 seconds of sustained hot contact triggers a cartoon 3.5-second charred wobble, convulsions, flames and smoke. Burning/waiting animals leave the neighbor snapshot and audio emitter list. The body then disappears in a smoke puff, leaving ash for at most 60 active seconds.

After at least 14 active seconds, the lifecycle considers 32 stable replacement points 6.5–8.5 tiles away from the volcano. Admission requires resident valid ground, species habitat, lava clearance and an off-screen margin of 220 logical pixels. If every point is visible or unavailable, replacement waits 2–3 seconds before retrying; the delay varies by animal to spread the work. Thus camping at a wide view can delay replenishment. The new animal travels toward its original home point, keeps species, appearance, group/leader/mother IDs, and starts with registered previous samples. The old record is reused: population cannot grow with repeated deaths. One ash pile per record bounds lingering effects.

Replacement excursions expand that record's territory to a bounded ten-tile ring, but ownership stays with the original chunk. Family members may therefore reappear at different times; ordinary following/waiting resumes after the return intent. There is no transfer into neighboring chunk records, permanent death history, reproduction, or ecology simulated beyond the active region.

## Checkpoints and limits

World/agent schemas are **21/17**. Older records fail explicitly. Nested animal state, original replacement bytes, lifecycle clocks, exposure, cycle count, ash, search retry clocks, encounter slots/targets/timeouts and family links serialize through registry codecs. Active state flushes before checkpointing. Sleeping wildlife resumes its saved lifecycle without advancing; expired chunks regenerate their initial population. Volcanic surface/eruption cycles use injected global world time. The screen visibility policy belongs to presentation and must be supplied by headless callers that want replacement admission (`world.spawnHidden`); it is not stored in checkpoints.

The single atlas is 4096×4068 (63.56 MiB decoded), within its 4096 × 4096 / 64 MiB ceiling. The packer reclaims vertical space beneath shorter shelf entries to fit the finer artwork without reducing other sprites. It retains exact frame deduplication and one-pixel transparent gutters. Template bytes and nested active state are included in managed memory estimates; cache budgets and the 144-active-chunk limit remain unchanged. Stable active-chunk iteration preserves simulation order after reactivation.

The revision was also exercised for 120 simulated seconds at ten site/seed combinations with regular scene composition. No exception or hard freeze occurred in that run. The reported intermittent freeze has not been reproduced conclusively. Two potential stall sources are bounded now: failed lifecycle searches have serialized cooldowns, and flow geometry no longer rebuilds for alternating simulation/presentation times. A later frame exception produces a visible diagnostic and a Reload jungle button instead of silently leaving a frozen canvas. CPU benchmarks exclude GPU completion and browser scheduling.
